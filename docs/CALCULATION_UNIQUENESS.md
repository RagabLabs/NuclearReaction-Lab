# Calculation uniqueness audit

Q is only an auxiliary rest-mass balance for reaction preparation. No standalone Q, binding, separation-energy or decay-time calculator is presented here. The engine and interface are independent of the two previous projects.

| ID | Scientific question | Essential inputs | Principal output | Difference from other calculations | Shared operations allowed | Reason for independence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|01|Minimum incident lab T for a channel?|Incident/final rest masses, excitation, stationary target|Threshold scalar and NR comparison|Accessibility bound, not product trajectory|Mass sums and invariant s|Answers minimum-energy boundary before any angle choice|
|02|How much collision energy is relative motion?|Incident masses, both kinetic energies, opening angle|Total W, initial KCM, invariant s, boost|Requires no final channel, supports moving targets|On-shell energy and vector sums|Analyzes incident state independent of threshold/outgoing state|
|03|What kinetic energy results from a given impulse?|Nucleus mass, initial p, applied q|Final T and signed ΔT, recoil speed|Impulse-to-energy map for arbitrary initial nucleus|Mass shell and vectors|No reaction selection or scattering-angle solution is needed|
|04|What complete two-body final states are allowed?|Four masses, beam T, angular condition|Both four-momenta, both energies/speeds, closure|Full exclusive final state rather than detector's one energy|CM state, Lorentz boost, root solver|Determines complete final momentum partition|
|05|Which directions correspond between frames?|Reaction state, angle and direction of mapping|Angles/preimages, maximum lab angle, geometry|Angular relation without density or detector-energy objective|Boost and lab roots|Resolves directional multiplicity and undefined mappings|
|06|Which energy would a detector at fixed lab angle see?|Reaction state and explicit lab detector angle|Allowed observed kinetic/total energies and branches|Detector readout only; no complete residual solution table|Same validated two-body roots|A distinct observational conditional problem with its own screen and main result|
|07|What momentum has been transferred?|Two momenta, scattering angle/frame, optional total energies|q vector/magnitude and optional invariant t|Difference along a selected incident/outgoing leg|Vectors and Minkowski metric|Transfer is not global event closure|
|08|Does an entered event close in momentum?|Arbitrary initial/final vector sets, common frame|Sum, residual, tolerance and pass/fail|Verification accepts arbitrary vectors; does not solve reaction or calculate q of a leg|Compensated vector addition|Independent data consistency question rather than trajectory prediction|
|09|How does supplied angular density change frame?|Reaction state, angle, branch-resolved densities|Jacobian, transformed values, lab branch sum or inverse|Needs supplied cross sections absent from pure kinematics|Angle map and Lorentz boost|Changes an angular probability-density measure, not energy or direction alone|
|10|What does a contact-Coulomb model estimate?|Charges, masses, R or r0, relative energy|VC, E/VC, Gamow and finite-radius WKB estimates|Requires model parameters and has approximate nuclear-dynamics limitations|Reduced mass, constants and energy units|A model barrier estimate independent of kinematic permission and supplied measured density|

Functions04 and06 deliberately share a physical solver: they ask different scientific questions and expose different primary results.04 reports the complete exclusive event;06 reports conditional detector energies and its energy-angle curve. Splitting root branches into separate calculators would be duplication and is not done.05 addresses angular mappings and09 transforms a supplied solid-angle density, requiring additional data and singular-density interpretation. No calculation was replaced to meet a cosmetic count.
